## Headline and history

[Epoch AI, 26 February 2026](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/hyperscaler-capex-trend) is the author of the cited SEC-based analysis. Its $770 billion is a **trend scenario for 2026**, covering all capital expenditure of Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle. It combines cash PP&E with new finance leases. The article explicitly says that the AI share is not disclosed. The previous claim that the vast majority is directed to AI was removed. This figure is not realised expenditure, current company guidance, or AI-only spending.

Confidence: high in transcription and interpretation; the future scenario itself is uncertain.

## AI evidence statements

All retained examples are dated historical observations, not claimed returns on the 2026 capex scenario.

- **Telecom:** removed the alleged AI benefit. The 2.7%/90%/10% figures mixed incompatible utilisation measures, and long-run cloud/streaming causality was not demonstrated by the cited Wikipedia page. Historical fibre is not itself an AI achievement.
- **AI revenue:** removed the associated 42% CAGR and “faster than any prior technology wave” claim with the unsupported row. The cited publisher does not substantiate the superlative.
- **AlphaFold:** [the database authors' 2024 paper](https://academic.oup.com/nar/article/52/D1/D368/7337620) supports over 214 million freely accessible **predicted** structures with confidence metrics. Removed the claim that the entire protein-folding problem has been solved. High confidence.
- **Fusion:** [Degrave et al., Nature, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-021-04301-9) supports RL control on EPFL's TCV research tokamak. Clarified the experimental milestone and attribution; it does not show commercial fusion. High confidence.
- **HungerMap:** [WFP's 16 April 2026 release](https://www.wfp.org/news/wfp-releases-hungermap-live-modernized-intelligence-platform-turns-data-global-hunger-early) gives monitoring in >50 countries but AI-assisted forecasts for **16 hotspots**. The former text applied forecast coverage to all countries and claimed an unsupported 90-day horizon. High confidence in WFP's reported coverage, not independent forecast accuracy.
- **Water leaks:** [Thames Water's 1 May 2026 release](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/news/2026/apr/thames-water-signs-landmark-leak-detection-contract-with-origin-tech-to-save-over-100-million-litres-of-water-per-day) confirms a project targeting 100 million litres/day savings. Replaced the 2024 date and attribution of all Origin technologies' reported 3B-litre impact exclusively to AI satellites. Clearly describe the new target as expected, not realised. High confidence in the announcement; no independent verified savings claim.
- **Drug discovery:** [Xu et al., Nature Medicine, 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-025-03743-2) documents a 71-person, 12-week phase 2a trial of rentosertib for lung fibrosis. Replaced the vendor's speed comparison with this current peer-reviewed milestone and the need for larger/longer trials. Explicitly not a malaria treatment. High confidence in trial details; no claim of proven clinical effectiveness or approval.
- **Cooling:** [DeepMind's 2016 primary report](https://deepmind.google/blog/deepmind-ai-reduces-google-data-centre-cooling-bill-by-40/) supports up to 40% **cooling-energy** reduction. Removed unsupported extrapolation to present-day grid optimisation and distinguished cooling from whole-site electricity. High confidence in the company's report, not independent validation.

## Interpretation limits and integration

The denominators are independently sourced benchmarks with different scopes and price years. Ratios illustrate scale; they cannot promise deployable projects, future annual budgets, or fungible government cash. None of the AI examples establishes a causal return on the displayed 2026 scenario. UI labels must preserve these distinctions in every locale. Removed data must not be recreated to satisfy a minimum row count.

Validation performed: JSON parsed, all historical sums recomputed, projection ratio reconciled, CPI calculation reproduced, seven retained rows have positive denominators and linked evidence. The AI dataset also passed the project Zod schema using Node’s native TypeScript loading. Whole-project build/UI verification belongs to the integration audit.

## Independent final non-AI fact-risk review

A second review of retained non-AI comparison evidence confirmed the exact historical cessation ($275.40), WHO 2024 TB ($797), WFP fundraising school meals ($50), WFP 2021 emergency plan ($0.43), IFAD ballpark small-farm climate finance ($75B), RAND Housing for Health median annual operating cost ($16,035), and May 2026 988 administration award ($255M). Their current methodology notes distinguish quoted prices, targets, study-era costs, and uncertain delivery outcomes. No additional removals were warranted for those rows.

One substantive scope refinement was applied to the gambling mental-health row: the $200B lower bound measures a gap in **domestic public financing** against the Lancet Commission budget-share targets (5% of health budgets for low- and middle-income countries, 10% for high-income countries). It is not a bottom-up costing of every mental-health need. The original report explicitly warns that targets are general estimates rather than national needs assessments. Added this basis and the [full UnitedGMH report](https://unitedgmh.org/app/uploads/2024/06/Financing-of-mental-health-V2.pdf) to the dataset. Evidence: report printed pp. 16–17 / PDF p. 9 explains target assumptions; printed pp. 28–29 / PDF p. 15 states the funding gap. Confidence: high for attribution and scope; the numerical estimate remains a historical lower bound.

The independent review subsequently retrieved the complete original vaccination paper through the [Europe PMC open-access full-text XML service](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC9850055/fullTextXML). Its Methods section explicitly specifies **2020 USD** for all costs. The article describes cumulative product, delivery and stockpile costs for 2021–2030, and does not describe discounting. Thus $269.8B / 10 = $26.98B remains an illustrative annual average, now with a verified 2020 price basis. This supersedes the earlier unverified-price-year caveat for vaccination; 2020 USD has been added to its three shared dataset rows and four-locale comparison labels, with the verified full-text link recorded alongside the publisher source. Confidence: high for price year, amount and period; no annual budget or universal vaccination guarantee is inferred.
